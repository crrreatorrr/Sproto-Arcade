## Setup

Use the same technical candidate and seed777 for each participant. Record the actual entry route and any remapped controls. Provide the standard control list (movement, jump, dash, shield, C/V, retry), but do not explain what the first marked front requires or coach its timing. Explain: “Please play aloud if comfortable. Tell me what you think the machine is about to do, what you will try, and why.” Avoid leading questions. The observer records behavior, including misunderstandings and failure causes, rather than correcting the player during a trial.

## Primary task: Marina first dash-only counter

1. Select Marina Phase2 practice. First let the player read the interface and pressure-pipe setup. Ask “What do you think this cue means?” before the first front reaches them. Do not mention a counter action beyond the controls already provided.
2. Count one **first-front trial** from fresh start until that first marked front is countered or missed. After a miss, ask the player to retry with R before another measured trial. This prevents several front cycles within one life from being mislabeled as attempt1. Count the first actual successful dash counter as1,2,3,more than3,or none observed.
3. Record cue identification separately: independent yes, partial/after prompting, or no. An observed primary success requires independent identification **and** a successful counter by trial3. Shielding, double-jumping or surviving damage alone does not count as the counter. Verify the game actually reports/opens the counter result.
4. After the measured first-counter trials, let the player explore the valve/vent objective. Ask “What changed in this phase, and what must you do to finish it?” Record their explanation and whether it was independently correct. Do not assume that a successful dash establishes Phase2 comprehension.

The planned target is at least6 of8 participants identifying and executing the first dash-only counter by attempt3. It remains pending until eight Marina primary observations exist. ## Failure and restart observations

Ask “What hit you or caused the fall?” Compare the player's account with the game's named source. Note ambiguous geometry, missed cues and surprising recovery. Ask “When did you feel you had control again?” Keep perceived delay separate from measured latency.

Only enter milliseconds if measured from R input to the first controlled movement, with the method recorded. A consented frame-count recording can support a measurement; divide frame count by recording rate and convert to milliseconds. Otherwise leave latency blank. The prototype target is under one second; a subjective “immediate” response is not proof of that timing.
